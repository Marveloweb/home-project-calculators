    How the carpet calculator works

    Wall-to-wall carpet is cut off a roll, usually 12 ft wide, so you buy whole widths, not just your floor area. The calculator lays strips of carpet across each section of your room the way an installer would. It tries the strips both ways, along the length and along the width, and keeps the one that uses less carpet. Each strip is cut 3 in longer at each wall for trimming, the minimum allowance in the Carpet and Rug Institute's residential installation standard (CRI 105), and rounded up to a whole foot.

    Strips = width covered ÷ roll width, rounded up
    Strip length = length + 3 in at each end, rounded up to a whole foot
    Carpet (sq ft) = strips × strip length × roll width
    Square yards = square feet ÷ 9
    Pad (sq yd) = floor area × 1.1 ÷ 9
    Tack strip = (room perimeter − 3 ft per doorway) × 1.1 ÷ 4 ft pieces

    Every strip runs the same way. CRI 105 requires adjacent pieces of the same carpet to have the pile in the same direction; turning a leftover sideways to fill a gap would show as a different shade along the seam. So the calculator never fills gaps with crosswise pieces, and your leftover is real: keep it for a closet or for repairs.

    Worked example

    A 15 by 12 ft bedroom has 180 sq ft of floor. On a 12 ft roll, one strip covers the 12 ft width, and it is cut 15 ft + 6 in = 15.5 ft, rounded to 16 ft. That is 16 × 12 = 192 sq ft, or 21.3 sq yd, with no seam. The pad is 180 × 1.1 ÷ 9 = 22 sq yd. The room is 54 ft around; less 3 ft for the doorway, 51 ft of tack strip with 10% extra is 15 strips of 4 ft.

    An 18 by 14 ft family room is wider than the roll. Strips along the 18 ft side would take 2 strips of 19 ft (38 ft off the roll); strips along the 14 ft side take 2 strips of 15 ft (30 ft). The calculator picks the second: 30 × 12 = 360 sq ft (40 sq yd) for 252 sq ft of floor, with a 14 ft seam. On a 15 ft roll the same room is one piece 19 ft long: 285 sq ft and no seam.

    Seams

    CRI 105 asks installers to keep seams to a minimum, run them the length of the room, keep them out of doorways and away from spots where people turn, and let traffic run along a seam rather than across it. When the calculator shows a seam, ask whether your carpet comes in a 13 ft 6 in or 15 ft width; a wider roll can remove the seam and sometimes costs less in total.

    Pad and tack strip

    The pad (cushion) is cut to the floor, so it is worked out from the floor area plus 10%. For stretched-in carpet in a home, CRI 105 says the pad should not be thicker than 7/16 in, and its seams should cross the carpet seams at right angles or sit at least 6 in to one side. Tack strip goes around the walls with a gap slightly less than the carpet thickness, never more than 3/8 in, and not across doorways, which get a transition strip instead.

    Before you buy

    • Measure each room at its widest points, into closets and to the middle of each doorway.
    • Draw a sketch with the direction you want the carpet to run, ideally with the pile facing the main entrance, as CRI 105 prefers, and take it to the store.
    • Buy all the carpet for connected rooms from one roll (one dye lot) so the color matches.
    • Stairs are cut separately from small pieces; measure them on their own and tell the store how many steps you have.

    Sources

    • Carpet and Rug Institute: CRI 105, Standard for Installation of Residential Carpet (PDF), sections 5.1 to 5.3 (seams, pile direction, 3 in trimming allowance), 11.1 (tack strip) and 11.2 (cushion)
    • This Old House: How to measure for carpet replacement, for measuring to the middle of doorways and the 10% overage used for pad

    Common questions

    How much carpet do I need for a 12 by 12 room?

    A piece 12 ft wide and 13 ft long off a 12 ft roll: 156 sq ft, or 17.3 sq yd. The extra foot of length is the 3 in trim allowance at each wall, rounded up to a whole foot.

    Why do I need more carpet than my room's square footage?

    Carpet is cut from a roll of fixed width, usually 12 ft. You pay for the full width of every strip, even the part trimmed off, plus at least 3 in along each wall for trimming, as the Carpet and Rug Institute's installation standard requires.

    How many square feet are in a square yard of carpet?

    9 square feet. Divide square feet by 9 to get square yards.

    Can leftover carpet be turned sideways to fill a gap?

    Not in the same room. The CRI installation standard requires every piece of the same carpet to have the pile running the same way, or the seam shows as a change in color. Leftovers can go in a closet or another room.

    How much carpet pad do I need?

    The floor area plus about 10%, in square yards. Pad seams should cross the carpet seams at right angles or sit at least 6 in to one side, according to CRI 105.
