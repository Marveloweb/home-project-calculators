How the countertop calculator works

Stone countertops are priced by the square foot but cut from whole slabs, and laminate and butcher block are sold in fixed lengths. The calculator works out both: the square feet of counters, island and backsplash, and what you have to buy to get them.

Counter (sq ft) = total run length × depth (in) ÷ 12
Island (sq ft) = island length × island depth (in) ÷ 12
Backsplash (sq ft) = total run length × backsplash height (in) ÷ 12
Laminate or butcher block = the shortest 4, 6, 8, 10 or 12 ft piece that covers each run

Measure the first run along the wall to its end. Measure each run that turns a corner from the front edge of the first run, so the corner square is not counted twice. Sink and cooktop holes are not taken off: the slab is bought and cut whole, and the cutouts are usually charged on top.

Counting slabs

For quartz and granite, the calculator lays your pieces out on slabs the way a fabricator's layout does, in simplified form. It keeps 1 in clear of each slab edge for the saw, splits any piece longer than the slab into equal halves (a seam), lines pieces up end to end in strips along the slab, and fits strips of different widths side by side across it, largest first. Two 25 1/2 in counter pieces fit across a standard 56.5 in slab with a 4 in backsplash strip beside them; a 36 in island beside a counter piece needs a jumbo slab.

Treat the slab count as a planning figure. A fabricator may need another slab to match the veining across a seam, to avoid a flaw in the stone, or for a thicker built-up edge, and granite slab sizes vary by quarry.

Worked example

An L-shaped kitchen has a 10 ft run and an 8 ft run, 25 1/2 in deep, a 4 in backsplash and a 6 ft island 36 in deep.

  • Counters: 18 ft × 25.5 ÷ 12 = 38.25 sq ft
  • Island: 6 ft × 36 ÷ 12 = 18 sq ft
  • Backsplash: 18 ft × 4 ÷ 12 = 6 sq ft
  • Total: 62.25 sq ft of quartz, which a fabricator would price as 63 sq ft or more

On standard slabs (118 by 54.5 in once the edges are kept clear), the 10 ft run and its backsplash are longer than the slab and are split into two 60 in pieces each. The 25 1/2 in pieces need three strips: two fill one slab side by side and the third starts another. The island fills a third slab, with all three backsplash strips beside it. That is 3 standard slabs. Jumbo slabs fit the 10 ft run in one piece, and the island and one counter strip side by side (36 + 25.5 = 61.5 in), so the same kitchen takes 2 jumbo slabs.

In laminate, the same kitchen takes a 10 ft and an 8 ft piece for the runs and, because a 36 in island is wider than 25 in stock, two 6 ft pieces side by side for the island (or one wider island top).

Before you buy

  • Get the final price from the fabricator's template, measured after the cabinets are installed and level.
  • Ask what the price per square foot includes: edge profile, sink and faucet holes, backsplash, tear-out of the old tops and plumbing reconnection are often extra.
  • Buy laminate pieces a little longer than the run; you can trim the end, but not add to it.
  • Seal butcher block on every face before installing, and fix it with slotted brackets so it can move with the seasons.
