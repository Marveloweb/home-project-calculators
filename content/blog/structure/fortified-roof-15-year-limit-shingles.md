If your home has a **FORTIFIED** roof, or you've been thinking about getting one for the insurance discount, a rule change is coming. Starting **January 1, 2027**, the Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety (IBHS), which runs the FORTIFIED program, will limit how long an **asphalt shingle** roof can keep qualifying for the designation: **15 years**.

The change has been in the roofing and insurance trade press for weeks, and in early November the Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association (ARMA) publicly challenged it. Here's what's changing, who it affects and what to do about it.

## What FORTIFIED is

FORTIFIED is a voluntary construction standard from IBHS, a research group funded by the insurance industry. The program started in Alabama in 2011, and more than 100,000 homes have now been designated. In several Southern states, insurers give premium discounts for a FORTIFIED roof. Homepros.news reports some discounts as high as 55 percent, though the size varies a lot by state and insurer, and some insurers offer none.

A FORTIFIED Roof goes beyond a typical reroof. According to IBHS's FORTIFIED site and GAF's program page, it requires:

- **Ring-shank nails** in an enhanced pattern instead of smooth nails. IBHS says they nearly double the roof's strength against wind.
- A **sealed roof deck**, so that water doesn't pour in through the seams between sheathing panels if shingles blow off. IBHS says this can cut water intrusion by up to 95%.
- A **wider drip edge** and a **fully adhered starter strip** at the edges.
- **High-wind shingles** rated ASTM D3161 Class F or ASTM D7158 Class H.
- **Wind- and rain-resistant attic vents.**

The work has to be done by a FORTIFIED-certified roofer and checked by a certified FORTIFIED evaluator. Designations generally have to be renewed every five years, with an inspection and paperwork.

## What changes on January 1, 2027

According to Roofing Contractor's coverage of the IBHS bulletin, the new limit applies to homes with asphalt shingles on all or part of the required roof areas, under FORTIFIED Roof, Silver and Gold. Metal and tile roofs aren't affected. How the 15 years is counted depends on your situation:

| Your situation | How long the roof can stay eligible |
| --- | --- |
| New FORTIFIED roof | Up to 15 years from the first designation date |
| Existing roof, installation date documented | Up to 15 years from when the roof was installed |
| Existing roof, installation date unknown | One term of up to five years, then no re-designation until the shingles are replaced |
| Designated before Jan. 1, 2027 | Keeps its current expiration date |

Once an all-shingle roof hits the limit, the whole roof has to be replaced and documented under the current FORTIFIED New Roof requirements to qualify again.

IBHS is careful to say the rule is only about the program. In the bulletin's words, "It does not establish asphalt shingle service life, determine whether an individual roof is fit for continued use." A 16-year-old roof can still be a perfectly good roof. It just can't carry the FORTIFIED label anymore.

IBHS also told Roofing Contractor that fewer than 50 homes will reach the 15-year limit in the next three years, so few people will feel it right away.

## Why IBHS did it, and why shingle makers object

The limit comes from a 10-year IBHS study that weathered shingles at test sites in South Carolina, Wisconsin and Ohio. IBHS found that most shingles begin losing wind resistance between **5 and 10 years** of exposure, and it named **unsealing** (the adhesive strip between shingle courses letting go) as the leading cause of wind vulnerability. It also found that product wind ratings didn't reliably predict how a shingle would perform after years outdoors.

ARMA says the study is too narrow to support an age cap. It points out that IBHS tested 14 products from seven manufacturers, a small slice of the market, and it questions the test method and the modeling. ARMA also notes that some samples showed no decline between years five and ten, and argues that installation details like nail placement and deck moisture matter as much as age. It's urging insurers and regulators not to adopt broad age-based limits.

Contractors interviewed by Claims Journal worry the rule could spread to insurers' general underwriting. That fear has history: Florida, Texas and Virginia already have laws about how insurers can treat roof age at renewal.

## What to do now

1. **Find your roof's installation date.** Look for the permit, the roofer's invoice, the manufacturer warranty registration or your home inspection report. Under the new rule, a documented date can buy you up to 15 years instead of a single five-year term.
2. **Check your designation certificate.** If it was issued before 2027, it keeps its current expiration date.
3. **Do the math.** A FORTIFIED shingle roof installed in 2014 runs out of eligibility in 2029. If you're near that point, ask your insurer what the discount is worth to you before deciding when to reroof.
4. **Ask about other materials.** The limit only applies to asphalt shingles, so get a metal roof quote if you're comparing long-term costs.
5. **Measure before you call.** Knowing your roof's size lets you sanity-check quotes.

## How big is your roof?

Roofs are priced by the **square**, which is 100 square feet of roof surface. Here's a typical ranch house: a 48 × 28 ft gable roof with 12-inch overhangs. With the overhangs, the footprint is 50 × 30 ft, or 1,500 sq ft. Our [roofing calculator](/roofing-calculator/) multiplies that by a pitch factor and adds 10% waste for a gable roof (15% for a hip):

| Pitch | Pitch factor | Roof area | Squares to buy (with 10% waste) | Bundles (3 per square) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4/12 | 1.054 | 1,581 sq ft | 17.4 | 53 |
| 6/12 | 1.118 | 1,677 sq ft | 18.4 | 56 |
| 8/12 | 1.202 | 1,803 sq ft | 19.8 | 60 |

The same house needs about **160 linear feet of drip edge** around the eaves and rakes, or 16 ten-foot pieces. A FORTIFIED job calls for a wider drip edge, so make sure the quote lists it.

These are estimates for a simple roof. Dormers, valleys and skylights add material. A quote that comes in well above your squares may include extra waste, tear-off of more than one layer, or new decking, so ask.

> **Fun fact:** Roofers price by the square, but your roof is always bigger than your house's footprint. Every inch of rise adds surface: a 6/12 roof has about 12% more area than the ground it covers, and a steep 12/12 roof has about 41% more. On our 1,500 sq ft example, that's the difference between 16.8 and 21.2 squares before waste.
