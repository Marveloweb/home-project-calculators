If you've been putting off a new fence or deck because lumber felt expensive, there's some news worth knowing. On **October 14, 2026**, the US Department of Commerce announced lower duties on most softwood lumber imported from Canada. Trade groups and builders have been talking about it ever since.

The cut is real, but it's smaller than the headlines make it sound, and it won't show up on a lumberyard receipt overnight. Here's what changed and how to use it when you plan a project.

## What changed

Commerce finished its **seventh administrative review** of the antidumping and countervailing duties on Canadian softwood lumber. The review covers lumber shipped in 2024. According to Global Affairs Canada, the new combined rate for most Canadian producers is **24.75%** (10.64% antidumping plus 14.11% countervailing). That's down from **35.16%**.

A few big mills got their own rates:

| Producer | New combined duty rate |
| --- | --- |
| West Fraser | 20.02% |
| Resolute | 25.55% |
| Canfor | 31.11% |
| Most other producers | 24.75% |

The new rates took effect when the notices were published in the Federal Register on **October 19**, according to Weekly Real Estate News.

## The catch: a 10% tariff still applies

There's a second charge on top of those duties. Since October 14, 2025, a separate **10% Section 232 tariff** has applied to softwood lumber imports, and it hasn't changed. The National Lumber and Building Material Dealers Association (NLBMDA) puts the combined burden at **34.75%**, down from **45.16%** under the old rates.

So for every $100 of Canadian lumber at its declared import value, the charges drop from about $45 to about $35. That's a meaningful change for importers. But it applies to the import value, not to the price on the shelf, and not all lumber sold in the US comes from Canada.

## Will fence and deck lumber get cheaper?

Maybe, slowly. Lumber prices depend on a lot more than duties. Weekly Real Estate News notes that the cut alone doesn't translate into a specific dollar saving, since what builders pay depends on wholesale prices, inventories, contracts, transportation and supplier competition.

The National Association of Home Builders (NAHB) explains that even when mill prices fall, retail relief lags. As NAHB's Jesse Wade puts it, "it may take at least a few weeks to a couple of months for builders to see price relief."

Prices were already fairly calm going in. NAHB reports that framing lumber averaged **$494.51 per 1,000 board feet** on October 23 (Madison's Lumber Price Index), down 2.4% from a month earlier and down 3.4% from a year earlier.

One more thing: the duties only cover softwood lumber. Posts, rails, pickets and deck framing made from wood are in that category. Composite decking and vinyl fencing aren't wood, so this news doesn't affect their price directly.

## Price your project with a materials list, not a guess

The best way to take advantage of easing prices is to know exactly what you need. Then you can compare quotes line by line and see whether a store's prices have actually moved. Here are two worked examples from our calculators.

### A 100-foot privacy fence

Assumptions: 6 feet tall, posts every 8 feet, 3 rails per section, 5.5-inch pickets with ½-inch gaps, and post holes 10 inches wide and 30 inches deep. These match the [fence calculator](/fence-calculator/) defaults, with a third rail added because taller fences often use one.

- **14 posts**, each **10 feet** long (6 feet above ground plus 30 inches buried, rounded up to a stock length)
- **13 sections** and **39 rails**
- **200 pickets**
- **1,200 fasteners** (two per picket at each rail)
- **30 bags** of 80 lb concrete mix for the posts, with 10% extra

The posts, rails and pickets are the softwood part of that list, so those are the line items to watch on a quote.

### A 12 × 16 ft deck

Assumptions: 5.5-inch-wide deck boards with ⅛-inch gaps, 16-foot boards running the long way, joists 16 inches on center and 10% extra for waste. Here's what the [deck calculator](/deck-calculator/) gives:

- **192 sq ft** of deck surface
- **26 rows** of boards, **416 linear feet** in all
- **29 deck boards**, 16 feet long
- **13 joists**, 12 feet long
- About **676 deck screws** (two per board at each joist)

The joists are almost always pressure-treated softwood, even under a composite deck. If you choose wood deck boards too, nearly the whole list is affected by lumber prices.

> **Fun fact:** NAHB says an average new single-family home uses roughly 15,000 board feet of framing lumber. A 16-foot 5/4 × 6 deck board is 10 board feet (using nominal sizes), so the 29 boards on that 12 × 16 deck add up to about 290 board feet. That one house's framing equals the board footage of deck boards for more than 50 decks that size. Run your own deck size through the [deck calculator](/deck-calculator/) to see your board count.

## Tips for buying this winter

- **Get your list first.** Run the numbers, then ask two or three suppliers to quote the same list. It's the easiest way to spot who has passed savings along.
- **Ask where the lumber comes from.** Your yard can tell you whether its stock is domestic or Canadian. Canadian stock is the part this duty change touches.
- **Don't wait forever.** Duties are reviewed every year (Canada says the eighth review started in March 2026), so rates can move in either direction. A fair price on a project you need now beats a guess about next year.
- **Remember concrete and hardware.** Bags of mix, screws and hangers aren't covered by the lumber duties, so price those separately.

## The bottom line

The October cut lowered duties on most Canadian softwood lumber from 35.16% to 24.75%, but the 10% tariff remains, so the total is still about 35%. Any savings will reach stores gradually, if at all. Measure your fence line or deck now, get a precise materials list, and use it to compare quotes over the next few weeks.
